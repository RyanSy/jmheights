# **What We Do**

\[BUILD SPEC: 4-up grid on desktop, 2x2 on tablet, stacked on mobile.\]

## **AC System Not Cooling**

Loss of cooling can come from a failed capacitor, frozen evaporator coil, refrigerant leak, thermostat issue, tripped breaker, or electrical failure. We diagnose the root problem before quoting repair options.

## **Frozen Coils and Ice on Refrigerant Lines**

Ice buildup on the indoor coil or copper refrigerant lines is usually caused by airflow restriction or low refrigerant. We identify the underlying cause and correct it properly.

## **Refrigerant Leak Detection and Repair**

Air conditioning systems do not consume refrigerant. Low charge means a leak exists somewhere in the system. We pressure-test, electronically detect leaks, and quote repair versus replacement honestly.

## **Electrical and Control Repairs**

Capacitors, contactors, blower motors, fan motors, thermostats, relays, and control boards are among the most common AC failures. Most repairs can be completed during the same visit.

# **Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel with 4 to 5 short tip blocks.\]

## **Check the Breaker First**

A tripped breaker is one of the most common reasons an AC system suddenly stops running. Reset it once only. If it trips again immediately, stop and call for service.

## **Replace the Air Filter**

A clogged air filter restricts airflow and can freeze the indoor evaporator coil. If the system iced up, allow several hours for thawing before restarting.

## **Listen to the Outdoor Unit**

A humming condenser fan usually points to a failed capacitor. Clicking without startup often points to the contactor. Total silence usually indicates a power or control issue.

## **Pay Attention to the Timeline**

Gradual cooling loss often suggests a refrigerant leak. Sudden complete failure usually indicates an electrical or component failure. The timeline helps narrow the diagnosis faster.

## **Continuous Runtime Is Not Normal**

On a typical 90-degree North Jersey day, a properly working AC system should reach setpoint within roughly an hour. Endless operation without cooling is a sign of a larger issue.

# **Before You Try It Yourself**

\[BUILD SPEC: Caution-styled panel.\]

## **Safe to Do Yourself**

- Replace the air filter

- Reset the breaker once

- Confirm thermostat settings

- Clear debris around the condenser

- Gently rinse the outdoor coil with a garden hose (power off first)

## **Stop and Call Us**

- Opening the outdoor condenser cabinet

- Working inside the air handler

- Replacing capacitors or motors

- Handling refrigerant or gauges

- Diagnosing control boards or wiring

## **Why This Matters in New Jersey**

HVAC repair involving refrigerant and electrical work requires licensed and EPA-certified service. Unlicensed work can void equipment warranties, create safety issues, and cause larger repair costs later.
