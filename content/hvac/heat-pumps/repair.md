# **What We Do**

\[BUILD SPEC: 4-up grid on desktop, 2x2 on tablet, stacked on mobile.\]

## **Defrost Cycle Diagnosis**

A heat pump that stays iced over, repeatedly enters defrost mode, or never fully clears frost has a control, sensor, or airflow problem. We diagnose the outdoor coil sensor, defrost logic, fan operation, and airflow path to locate the actual fault.

## **Reversing Valve and Refrigerant Repair**

The reversing valve controls whether the system heats or cools. A stuck valve, leaking valve, or incorrect refrigerant charge causes major performance issues in both modes. We recover, repair, recharge, and test the system to manufacturer specifications.

## **Sensor and Control Board Diagnosis**

Modern heat pumps rely heavily on sensors and control boards. Faulty outdoor temperature sensors, discharge sensors, indoor coil sensors, or failed boards create inconsistent operation and error codes. We identify the failed component before replacing parts.

## **Auxiliary Heat Strip Repair**

Most North Jersey heat pumps use electric resistance backup heat during extreme cold. Failed heat strips, sequencers, relays, or incorrect thermostat settings can leave the house cold even when the heat pump appears operational.

# **Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel with 4 to 5 short tip blocks.\]

## **Write Down the Error Code**

Most modern heat pumps display fault codes on the indoor unit, thermostat, or outdoor board. The error code plus the model number helps us bring the right parts on the first visit.

## **Some Frost Is Normal**

Heat pumps naturally build frost during winter operation and periodically enter defrost mode to clear it. A unit completely buried in ice for hours is not normal and typically points to a defrost failure or airflow issue.

## **Auxiliary Heat Should Not Run Constantly**

If your thermostat constantly displays AUX or Emergency Heat, the heat pump itself may not be operating efficiently. Backup electric heat costs significantly more to run and usually indicates a larger system issue.

## **Check the Air Filter First**

Dirty filters restrict airflow and trigger multiple heat-pump faults, including frozen coils, low pressure, and reduced heating output. Replacing the filter solves more service calls than most homeowners realize.

## **Older Heat Pumps Perform Differently**

Pre-2018 heat pumps often lose heating capacity during colder NJ weather. Modern cold-climate systems maintain performance much better at low outdoor temperatures. Sometimes replacement makes more financial sense than continued repair.

# **Before You Try It Yourself**

\[BUILD SPEC: Caution-styled panel.\]

## **Safe to Do Yourself**

- Replace the air filter

- Power-cycle the unit at the breaker

- Clear leaves, snow, or debris around the outdoor unit

- Read and report the error code

- Confirm the thermostat is set to HEAT instead of Emergency Heat

## **Stop and Call Us**

- Opening refrigerant service ports

- Working inside the outdoor unit cabinet

- Replacing capacitors, sensors, or boards

- Adding refrigerant

- Chipping ice off the outdoor coil with tools
