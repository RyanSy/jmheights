# **What We Do**

## **Manual J Load Calculation**

Air conditioning should never be sized by square footage alone. Manual J calculations account for insulation, windows, ductwork, ceiling height, air leakage, orientation, and occupancy to determine the correct cooling load.

## **Matched Indoor and Outdoor Equipment**

The condenser, evaporator coil, furnace, or air handler are selected as a manufacturer-approved matched system to achieve rated efficiency and preserve warranty eligibility.

## **Variable-Speed and Two-Stage Equipment**

Single-stage systems operate fully on or fully off. Two-stage and inverter-driven systems run quieter, maintain more even temperatures, and control humidity more effectively. We explain the cost and performance trade-offs clearly.

## **Permitting and Rebate Filing**

Township permits, inspections, manufacturer warranty registration, and NJ Clean Energy Program rebate paperwork are handled as part of the installation process.

# **Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel with 4 to 5 short tip blocks.\]

## **Bigger Is Not Better**

Oversized AC systems short-cycle, cool unevenly, and leave homes humid. Proper sizing is one of the most important parts of the installation.

## **SEER2 Is the Current Efficiency Standard**

Modern AC systems are rated under the newer SEER2 efficiency standard. Higher-efficiency systems may qualify for NJ Clean Energy rebates depending on equipment tier.

## **Existing Ductwork Matters**

A new AC system installed onto undersized or leaking ductwork will never perform correctly. Duct condition and airflow are assessed during the quote process.

## **Equipment Placement Affects Performance**

Outdoor condensers require proper airflow and service access. Indoor coils require correct drainage and airflow routing. Equipment location matters for efficiency and long-term serviceability.

## **Replace Indoor and Outdoor Components Together**

Installing a new outdoor condenser onto an aging indoor coil often reduces efficiency, shortens lifespan, and may void warranty coverage.

# **Before You Try It Yourself**

## **Safe to Do Yourself**

- Gather utility bills for load analysis

- Photograph existing HVAC equipment and ductwork

- Research SEER2 ratings and rebates

- Identify preferred condenser locations

- Check local township permit requirements

## **Stop and Call Us**

- Refrigerant handling or line-set work

- Electrical wiring and disconnect installation

- Duct system modifications

- Thermostat wiring

- Permit applications requiring contractor licensing

## **Why This Matters in New Jersey**

Central AC installation requires licensed HVAC work, EPA-certified refrigerant handling, and township permits. Proper installation preserves warranty coverage, qualifies for rebates, and ensures the system performs to specification.
