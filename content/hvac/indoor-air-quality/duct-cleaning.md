## **Camera Inspection Before Cleaning**

We inspect the supply trunks, return lines, and branch runs with a duct camera before recommending service. If the system does not need cleaning, we say so.

## **NADCA-Standard Whole-System Cleaning**

HEPA-filtered negative-air machines remove loosened debris while rotary brushes clean the interior surfaces of the duct system branch by branch. Cleaning follows National Air Duct Cleaners Association standards.

## **Coil and Blower Cleaning**

Dust and debris buildup often accumulates most heavily on the evaporator coil and blower assembly. Proper duct cleaning includes these components so the entire HVAC system is cleaned together.

## **Dryer Vent Cleaning**

Dryer vent cleaning can be added during the same visit. Restricted dryer vents reduce efficiency and increase fire risk, especially in longer vent runs.

# **Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel with 4 to 5 short tip blocks.\]

## **Most Homes Do Not Need Routine Duct Cleaning**

The EPA does not recommend duct cleaning on a fixed schedule. The strongest reasons for cleaning include post-renovation debris, water damage, visible contamination, rodent activity, or persistent odors from the duct system.

## **Check the Registers First**

Removing a supply register and looking inside the duct with a flashlight gives a good first indication. Light dust is normal. Heavy buildup, visible debris, or musty odor may justify cleaning.

## **A One-Time Cleaning After Purchasing a Home Can Make Sense**

For older homes without documented HVAC maintenance history, a one-time cleaning establishes a clean baseline for the new homeowner.

## **Beware Extremely Cheap Duct Cleaning Offers**

Proper NADCA-standard duct cleaning requires specialized equipment and several hours of labor. Very low advertised pricing often leads to aggressive upselling or incomplete work.

## **Filter Maintenance Prevents Most Dust Issues**

Regular filter replacement is the best long-term way to keep duct systems cleaner and HVAC airflow healthier.

# **Before You Try It Yourself**

\[BUILD SPEC: Caution-styled panel.\]

## **Safe to Do Yourself**

- Vacuum visible supply and return grilles

- Replace HVAC filters regularly

- Inspect ducts visually with a flashlight

- Document odors, dust buildup, or airflow issues

- Clean accessible vent covers

## **Stop and Call Us**

- Attempting full duct cleaning with a shop vacuum

- Cutting access openings into ductwork

- Cleaning evaporator coils or blower assemblies

- Removing duct insulation

- Attempting mold remediation inside HVAC systems

## **Why This Matters in New Jersey**

Improper duct cleaning can spread dust throughout the home, damage ductwork, or create HVAC airflow problems. Proper cleaning requires negative-air containment, HEPA filtration, and correct access methods to protect both the HVAC system and the indoor environment.
