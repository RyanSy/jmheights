# **Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel with 4 to 5 short tip blocks.\]

## **Mini-Splits Work Best in Hard-to-Reach Spaces**

Additions, finished attics, garages, and converted basements are often difficult to cool with existing ductwork. A dedicated mini-split usually performs better and costs less than extending ducts.

## **Whole-Home Ductless Is Common in Older NJ Homes**

Homes with radiator heat or boiler systems often use multi-zone mini-splits instead of installing full ductwork throughout the house.

## **Cold-Climate Equipment Matters**

Standard heat pumps lose capacity during cold NJ winters. Cold-climate systems like Mitsubishi Hyper-Heat and Daikin Aurora continue producing heat into single-digit temperatures.

## **Indoor Unit Placement Makes a Difference**

Wall-mounted heads are most common, but ceiling cassettes and floor-mounted units may work better depending on room layout and aesthetics.

## **Ductless and Central Air Can Work Together**

Many homeowners use central air for the main living areas and mini-splits for additions, finished basements, or rooms with persistent comfort problems.

# **Before You Try It Yourself**

\[BUILD SPEC: Caution-styled panel.\]

## **Safe to Do Yourself**

- Identify rooms with uneven temperatures

- Photograph the proposed installation area

- Gather utility bills for sizing analysis

- Research cold-climate equipment options

- Confirm possible outdoor condenser locations

## **Stop and Call Us**

- Refrigerant handling or line-set installation

- Electrical connections and disconnects

- Wall penetrations for condensate and line sets

- Condensate drainage setup

- Township permit applications

## **Why This Matters in New Jersey**

Ductless mini-split installations involve licensed refrigerant work, electrical connections, and building-envelope penetrations. Improper installations commonly lead to refrigerant leaks, noisy operation, drainage problems, failed inspections, and voided manufacturer warranties.
