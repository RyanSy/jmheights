## **No Heat or Low Heat**

The most common winter boiler service call. The issue may involve the thermostat, circulator pump, aquastat, burner, low-water condition, or a failed zone valve. We diagnose the full system before recommending the repair.

## **Pilot, Ignition, and Burner Problems**

Pilot lights that will not stay lit, ignition systems that fail to fire, dirty burners, oil soot buildup, or faulty gas valves. Most common burner and ignition repairs are completed the same day.

## **Boiler and Heating Loop Leaks**

Leaks at relief valves, circulators, expansion tanks, piping, zone valves, or the boiler body itself. We pressure-test the system, locate the source, and recommend the proper repair path.

## **Banging, Knocking, and Boiler Noise**

Steam boiler water hammer, hot-water boiler air noise, kettling, or combustion whistle sounds all point to different issues. The sound itself often helps narrow the diagnosis quickly.

# **Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel with short tips.\]

## **Check the thermostat and power switch**

Make sure the thermostat is set to heat and the setpoint is above room temperature. Confirm the boiler service switch is on and the emergency shutoff has not been turned off accidentally.

## **Check the boiler pressure gauge**

Most residential hot-water boilers should read roughly 12–15 PSI when cold. Very low pressure may prevent the boiler from firing. Very high pressure can trigger the relief valve.

## **Look at the steam boiler sight glass**

Steam boilers need visible water in the gauge glass. If the glass is empty, the low-water cutoff may have shut the boiler down for safety.

## **Reset the oil burner once only**

If the burner locks out, press the reset button once. If it shuts down again immediately, stop and call us. Repeated resets can flood the combustion chamber with oil.

## **Note when the problem started**

A sudden no-heat issue points toward ignition or electrical failure. Weak heat over several days may point toward circulation, pressure, or maintenance issues.

# **Before You Try It Yourself**

\[BUILD SPEC: Caution-styled panel.\]

## **Safe to do yourself**

Check thermostat settings, confirm power switches are on, inspect the pressure gauge, check the steam boiler sight glass, and replace thermostat batteries if needed. Photograph the boiler and any visible leak before calling.

## **Stop and call us**

Do not open the boiler cabinet, work on the gas valve, bleed radiators if you are unfamiliar with the process, or continue resetting the burner repeatedly. Boiler systems combine combustion, pressurized water or steam, and electrical controls.
