# **H2: Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel, 5 short tip blocks.\]

### **Schedule maintenance before peak winter demand.**

September and October are the best times for heating tune-ups. Waiting until the first cold week usually means longer appointment windows and higher emergency demand.

### **Dirty filters are the most common preventable issue.**

Restricted airflow increases wear on furnaces and heat pumps, raises utility costs, and contributes to overheating or freeze-related failures.

### **Combustion analysis matters on gas equipment.**

A visual inspection alone does not confirm safe operation. Combustion testing verifies CO levels, venting performance, and fuel efficiency.

### **Maintenance helps preserve manufacturer warranties.**

Most furnace, boiler, and heat pump manufacturers require documented annual service to maintain warranty eligibility on major components.

### **Small issues become expensive winter breakdowns.**

Weak igniters, failing capacitors, leaking relief valves, or dirty burners are inexpensive repairs during maintenance visits and expensive emergency calls during January freeze conditions.

# **H2: Before You Try It Yourself**

\[BUILD SPEC: Caution-styled panel.\]

### **Safe to do yourself:**

Replace standard air filters regularly.\
Keep outdoor heat pump units free of leaves and debris.\
Confirm thermostat settings and battery condition.\
Keep the area around the furnace or boiler clean and unobstructed.\
Monitor for unusual noises, smells, or water leaks.

### **Stop and call us:**

Anything involving gas burners, combustion chambers, refrigerant, electrical controls, or venting systems.\
Boiler pressure or relief-valve issues.\
Heat exchanger inspection.\
Combustion analysis and gas-pressure adjustments.\
Internal cleaning of burners or blower assemblies.

### **Why this matters in NJ:**

Heating systems combine combustion, gas piping, electrical controls, airflow, and venting systems that directly affect safety and performance. Improper maintenance can lead to reduced efficiency, equipment damage, or dangerous carbon monoxide conditions. Professional maintenance confirms the system is operating safely before winter demand hits.
