# **H2: What We Do**

\[BUILD SPEC: 5-up grid on desktop, stacked on mobile.\]

### **No Heat or Short Cycling**

The most common winter call. Could be the thermostat, a tripped breaker, a flame sensor that needs cleaning, a dirty filter restricting airflow, a draft inducer or pressure switch failure, or a cracked heat exchanger. We diagnose to root cause before quoting.

### **Ignition and Pilot Failures**

Hot surface igniters that will not fire, electronic ignition systems that fail repeatedly, gas valves that will not open, and rolled-out flame at the burner. Most are same-day repairs from truck stock.

### **Blower Motor and Control Issues**

ECM blower motors, blower control boards, capacitors on PSC blowers, limit switches, and failed relays. The blower runs every heating cycle, so motor and control failures show up fast in winter.

### **Furnace Blowing Cold Air**

A furnace running but blowing cool or lukewarm air usually points to ignition failure, overheating from restricted airflow, blower timing problems, or a control issue. We isolate the cause before recommending repair or replacement.

### **Cracked Heat Exchanger Inspection**

A cracked heat exchanger is a serious safety issue because combustion gases, including carbon monoxide, can enter the air supply. We inspect with a camera and combustion analyzer when the symptoms point that way. A confirmed cracked exchanger means the furnace gets shut down and replaced.

# **H2: Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel, 5 short tip blocks.\]

### **Check the breaker, the switch, and the filter.**

A tripped HVAC breaker, the furnace power switch flipped off, and a clogged filter together account for a large percentage of no-heat calls. All three are quick checks before scheduling service.

### **Note the failure pattern.**

Furnace fires but shuts off after a few minutes usually points to a limit switch or airflow restriction. Furnace tries to ignite but fails points to the igniter, flame sensor, or gas valve. Furnace runs but no warm air points to blower or control issues. The pattern helps narrow the diagnosis faster.

### **Take CO alarms seriously.**

A carbon monoxide alarm during heating season points to combustion or venting trouble. Get everyone out, ventilate the home if safe, and call us. Do not restart the furnace until it has been inspected.

### **A sulfur or rotten-egg smell is gas.**

That is a gas leak signal, not a furnace repair issue. Leave the home immediately, avoid switches or open flames, and call PSE&G at 1-800-880-7734 from outside the home.

### **New noises matter.**

Banging, squealing, rattling, grinding, or booming sounds are usually early warning signs. Calling when the noise starts is almost always cheaper than waiting for full failure during a cold snap.

# **H2: Before You Try It Yourself**

\[BUILD SPEC: Caution-styled panel.\]

### **Safe to do yourself:**

Reset the HVAC breaker once.\
Check the furnace power switch.\
Replace the filter.\
Confirm the thermostat is set to heat and above room temperature.\
Clear snow or debris from intake/exhaust pipes on high-efficiency furnaces.\
Identify furnace make, model, and age from the rating plate.

### **Stop and call us:**

Anything inside the furnace cabinet. The components run on high voltage and gas or oil combustion.\
Anything involving the gas supply or gas valve.\
Cleaning the flame sensor yourself unless you know the procedure. Improper cleaning damages the sensor quickly.\
Resetting after a CO alarm or flame rollout condition. The cause has to be identified first.

### **Why this matters in NJ:**

Furnace repair involves combustion equipment, high-voltage electrical components, venting, and carbon monoxide safety. Done wrong, the result can be a CO hazard, gas leak, fire risk, or equipment damage. Furnace repair is licensed work in NJ for good reason, and properly performed work protects both the equipment warranty and the home's safety.

# **H2: Furnace Repair FAQs**

### **How fast can JM Heights get to my home?**

### **Why is my furnace blowing cold air?**

Common causes include ignition failure, overheating from restricted airflow, blower timing issues, thermostat problems, or a failing control board. We diagnose the exact cause before recommending repair.

### **Should I repair or replace my furnace?**

If the furnace is under 12 years old and the repair is minor, repair usually makes sense. Past 15 years, major repairs often favor replacement. We walk through the repair-versus-replacement math honestly before work begins.

### **How often should a furnace be serviced?**

Once per year before heating season. Annual maintenance improves efficiency, catches safety issues early, and helps preserve the manufacturer warranty.
