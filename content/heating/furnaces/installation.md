### **80 Percent Standard-Efficiency Furnace**

80 to 83 percent AFUE. Vents through the existing chimney, often with a properly sized liner added during installation. Lower install cost and simpler venting. Right answer for smaller homes, tighter budgets, or homes where chimney venting already works well.

### **96 Percent High-Efficiency Condensing Furnace**

95 to 98 percent AFUE. Sealed combustion with PVC sidewall venting and condensate drainage. Lower fuel bills, quieter operation, and NJ Clean Energy rebate eligibility. The right fit for most North Jersey replacement installs.

### **Two-Stage and Variable-Speed Furnaces**

Single-stage furnaces run full output or off. Two-stage systems operate on low and high fire. Variable-speed furnaces modulate output continuously for quieter operation, more even temperatures, and better humidity control.

### **Manual J Load Calculation**

Replacing the old furnace with the same BTU size is usually wrong. We run a fresh Manual J on every install because most NJ homes are better insulated today than when the original furnace was installed. Most replacements size smaller than the outgoing unit.

### **Furnace and AC System Matching**

The furnace blower also drives the air conditioning system. We verify the indoor coil, blower speed, duct sizing, and airflow setup so the heating and cooling systems operate correctly together.

# **H2: Helpful Tips Before You Call**

\[BUILD SPEC: Light-background panel, 5 short tip blocks.\]

### **Bigger is not better on a furnace.**

Oversized furnaces short-cycle, create uneven temperatures, run louder, and wear out faster. Proper Manual J sizing fixes the issue and improves comfort.

### **The chimney may need a liner.**

Replacing an older furnace with a newer high-efficiency model often changes the venting requirements. Existing chimneys are frequently oversized for modern equipment and require a properly sized liner.

### **Condensing furnaces vent differently.**

A 96 percent furnace vents through PVC pipe out the side wall instead of the chimney. The vent termination location, pipe slope, and condensate drainage all matter for safe operation.

### **Consider the AC system at the same time.**

The furnace shares the cabinet and blower with the AC evaporator coil. If the AC is also aging, replacing both systems together often saves labor and avoids airflow mismatch problems later.

### **Variable-speed blowers improve both heating and cooling.**

A variable-speed ECM blower runs quieter, distributes air more evenly, and improves AC humidity control during summer as well.

# **H2: Furnace Installation FAQs**

### **Should I choose an 80 percent or 96 percent furnace?**

A 96 percent condensing furnace saves more fuel and qualifies for rebates but requires PVC venting and condensate drainage. An 80 percent furnace is less expensive upfront and uses chimney venting. The right answer depends on the house layout, venting path, and budget.

### **How long does a furnace installation take?**

Most residential furnace installs take one full day. More complex jobs involving duct modifications, venting changes, or AC coil replacement can extend into a second day.

### **What size furnace does my home need?**

The size depends on insulation, windows, air leakage, ceiling height, ductwork, and the home's actual heat loss. We calculate sizing using Manual J rather than square footage alone.

### **Do new furnaces qualify for NJ rebates?**

Yes. Many high-efficiency systems qualify for NJ Clean Energy Program rebates depending on the AFUE rating and utility provider. We handle the paperwork for qualifying systems.
