# **Common Water Heater Repairs We Handle**

## **Pilot Light & Thermocouple Repair**

If the pilot light won’t ignite or repeatedly goes out, the issue is often related to the thermocouple, burner assembly, or gas control system. We diagnose and repair the problem safely and restore proper operation.

## **Thermostat & Heating Element Replacement**

Electric water heaters commonly lose hot water due to failed thermostats or burned-out heating elements. We test both components before replacing parts to ensure an accurate repair.

## **Sediment Flush & Tank Cleaning**

Popping, rumbling, or kettling sounds usually mean sediment has built up at the bottom of the tank. A professional flush helps restore efficiency, improve performance, and reduce strain on the system.

## **T&P Valve & Safety Component Repair**

Temperature and pressure relief valves, anode rods, dip tubes, and safety controls are inspected and tested during service calls to ensure safe operation.

## **Leak Diagnosis & Repair**

Leaks around fittings, shutoff valves, drain valves, or supply connections are often repairable. If the tank itself is leaking internally, replacement is usually required.

# **Helpful Tips Before You Call**

## **Listen for Unusual Sounds**

Rumbling or popping often points to sediment buildup. Hissing may indicate overheating or leaking. Some minor ticking noises are normal as pipes expand and contract.

## **Check the Age of the Unit**

Most water heaters last around 8 to 12 years. If your system is over 10 years old and starting to fail, replacement may be more cost-effective than repeated repairs.

## **Verify Power or Pilot Status**

For electric units, check whether the breaker has tripped. For gas systems, confirm whether the pilot light is out before scheduling service.

## **Watch for Water Around the Tank**

Leaks from valves or fittings may be repairable. Water pooling directly under the tank often indicates internal tank failure.

## **Keep the Thermostat at 120°F**

Higher temperatures increase energy usage, accelerate sediment buildup, and raise scalding risk without providing much added benefit.

# **Before You Try a DIY Repair**

## **Safe Basic Troubleshooting**

- Resetting a tripped breaker on an electric water heater

- Relighting a pilot light according to the manufacturer instructions

- Adjusting the thermostat temperature

- Checking for visible leaks around fittings

## **Stop & Call a Licensed Plumber For:**

- Gas valve or burner repairs

- Heating element replacement

- Tank draining with corroded valves

- T&P valve replacement

- Any gas smell or visible scorching

## **Why Professional Repair Matters**

Gas water heaters involve combustion, venting, gas connections, and pressure safety controls. Improper repairs can create serious safety risks including gas leaks, water damage, carbon monoxide exposure, or pressure failure.
