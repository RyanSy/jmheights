## **Pipe Bursting Sewer Replacement**

Pipe bursting replaces the existing sewer line by pulling a new pipe through the old one while simultaneously breaking apart the damaged pipe underground.

This method is commonly used when:

- The existing line is severely deteriorated

- Full replacement is needed

- Surface preservation is important

Pipe bursting typically requires only two access pits rather than a full trench across the property.

## **CIPP Sewer Pipe Lining**

Cured-in-place pipe lining creates a new pipe inside the existing sewer line using a resin-saturated liner that hardens in place.

CIPP lining works best for sewer lines that:

- Have cracks or root intrusion

- Are structurally weakened

- Still maintain overall pipe shape

- Are not fully collapsed

The result is a smooth new interior pipe with minimal disruption to the property.

# **Helpful Tips Before You Call**

## **Understand That Not Every Sewer Line Qualifies**

Trenchless methods work best on sewer lines that still maintain a reasonably continuous pipe path. Fully collapsed or severely misaligned pipes may still require excavation.

## **Compare Total Project Cost, Not Just Pipe Repair Cost**

Traditional excavation may appear less expensive initially, but driveway, landscaping, and hardscape restoration costs can significantly increase the total project cost.

## **Trenchless Systems Are Built for Long-Term Service**

Modern trenchless materials such as HDPE piping and CIPP liners are designed for decades of service life comparable to traditional sewer replacement materials.

## **Most Projects Are Completed Quickly**

Many residential trenchless sewer repairs can be completed within one to two days once permits and inspections are approved.

## **Permits & Utility Mark-Outs Are Still Required**

Trenchless sewer replacement still requires permits, inspections, and NJ Call Before You Dig (811) coordination before work begins.
